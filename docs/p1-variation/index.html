MiniMax H3: what the pass-1 solver does to the movie

Thirteen takes of one scene. Same prompt, same seed, same 1024×1024, same 124 frames, same checkpoint. The only thing that changes is the pass-1 sampler, scheduler and step count, and there is no second pass anywhere. We are curious which ones you like, and why.

Why we ran it: we froze a new pass 1 for a pass-2 sampler study and the clips came back visibly different from the scene we bench against. They were not a variation on it, they were a different take. So we measured it.

The one number that makes the rest readable

Re-running the exact recipe behind our shipped clip today, three weeks and a core update later, reproduces it at 39.9 dB — tighter than that clip's own re-encode (37.8 dB). So the pipeline is reproducible, and every other difference below is caused by the setting rather than by drift in the stack. Every other take lands 14-18 dB from the reference, i.e. further apart than two different pass-2 samplers on an identical pass 1 (23-26 dB). There appears to be no small change to pass 1: change any part of the solve and you get a different movie, not a nudged one.

Read the clips, not the table

drift is distance from the reference take, not quality. motion is mean frame-to-frame luminance difference, and on a lateral pan that is dominated by the camera, so it ranks gross energy and cannot see whether a character's turn reads well. That is the part we want eyes on. We have our own ordering and are deliberately not stating it here so as not to anchor yours.

What we already know it does not explain

Drift does not track preference: in our own viewing the two furthest-drifting takes sit at opposite ends of the ranking. Step count does not behave uniformly either — res_multistep + simple and er_sde + beta gain gross motion as steps rise, while both gradient_estimation recipes lose it. One scene, one seed, so treat all of that as a starting point rather than a result.
