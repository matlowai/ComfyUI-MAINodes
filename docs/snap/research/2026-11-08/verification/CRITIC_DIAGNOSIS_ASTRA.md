## Where the source guidance itself needs caution

The scale reading rule says boards/walk tests use an eye-height camera and permits the far ground line as a horizon cue. In a generated image, a distant snow boundary or house base is not automatically a calibrated vanishing line, and the empty plate does not prove camera height. This encourages overconfident scale diagnoses when the assumed camera is low or projection is inconsistent. Here the existing scorer's historical calls are preserved; no scale score is changed. A future separately measured calibration revision should distinguish verified pose/anchors from a plausible camera hypothesis and retain uncertainty. The previously rejected r7 horizon experiment is not evidence that another wording tweak will succeed.

X14 also illustrates a bounded packet-contract issue: the fixture supplies one frame labeled as a board while its source is a video take. Correctly admitting unavailable temporal/audio evidence is good behavior. It should not be treated as a completed live cut critique.
