# Lighthouse set review — Astra, 2026-11-08

Read-only design consultation under SNAP-R269/R271. No generation, source/runtime changes, canon adoption or docket verdict. This review owns only this report; the collector owns source files and its inventory.

The old example's architectural premise needs correction before more angles. It places an optic inside a mostly opaque domestic room, with one small window, so the pictured enclosure does not explain the outward light path expected of the named lighthouse lantern. Point Reyes provides a documented alternative with separate clockwork and glazed lantern levels. It is architectural evidence, not an automatic choice of location or period for the fictional north lighthouse. This review does not assert that every lighthouse has a round plan.

## Features to carry if this documented case informs the design

Use one coordinate frame centred on the lens, with the stair arrival and identified glazing bays as fixed landmarks. Keep exterior compass directions attached to the documented source; do not transfer the old fictional east-window coordinates to Point Reyes.

The case's essential relationships are: a sixteen-sided iron structure; continuous segmented glazing at the lantern level; the first-order lens centrally above its clockwork; peripheral circulation and internal stair connection; exterior gallery/deck and railing beyond the glazing; and the conical roof with its structural rods, central chimney/ventilation arrangement and top ventilator. The lens is a large prism-and-bull's-eye assembly, not the old compact cylindrical brass lantern. Sheets 2–5 explain roof, glazing, vents, deck/hatch and stair details. Preserve those relationships before decorative weathering.

Do not turn this into a universal material recipe: the 1990 description records resilient tiles over evidence of wood at the clockwork floor, while the 2020 frame visibly shows wood. Nor does the evidence establish the fictional desk, radio, portable lantern, time of day or actor positions. These are production choices. The HABS sheets warn that insufficient information at editing may prevent conformance with HABS/HAER standards; retain that caveat and do not label them a certified current as-built.

## Staging and coverage to test after the packet is complete

A plausible upper-lantern scene would put Ferro beside an identified glazing bay, looking seaward, with Kon arriving by the documented stair and stepping onto visible usable floor. The optic supplies a meaningful foreground obstruction and can hide/reveal Kon. A two-shot can look tangentially along the aisle, with the two characters on the same accessible side of the optic; it should not require seeing through the lens body. Singles and a matched reverse should retain a specific mullion, stair edge or lens support as an anchor. Establish the actor-to-actor line and camera marks before deciding that a screen-side swap is wrong. These are coverage proposals, not proof that the currently unseen reverse fits.

Alternatively, the door/letter exchange can occur on the clockwork level, with a separate ascent and seaward beat upstairs. That preserves the building's distinct functions but changes the old beat's staging. A desk/radio workspace elsewhere is another explicit fictional design choice. None is silently adopted here.

Keep the cold sea ambience and warm lamp effect as a proposed look; the present sources do not verify the old amber sweep, a particular beam visibility in air, or dusk exposure. Put any world-side lighting decision on the future location record, then let reverse cameras change its screen-side appearance.

Next assets: first, one clear installed-lens interior reverse that reveals stair arrival and floor; second, a clean textured top/bird's-eye of the chosen production layout, checked against plan and genuine sides. Use separate tops if both levels are staged. Keep the schematic plan separately, with actor/camera marks and explicit unknowns. Before constructing those assets, settle whether the fiction adopts this arrangement or another documented lighthouse; collecting Point Reyes did not make that canon. No exact metres or complete camera feasibility are claimed from these perspective pictures.
