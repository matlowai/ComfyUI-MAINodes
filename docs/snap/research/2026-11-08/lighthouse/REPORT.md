# Point Reyes lantern-room reference packet

Read `contact.html` (local visual review; media is not shipped in this evidence copy) and the [independent Astra review](SET_REVIEW_ASTRA.md) for the uncropped visual review and [SOURCES.json](SOURCES.json) for exact identifiers, URLs, dimensions, byte sizes and SHA-256 inventory. All source downloads were preserved without overwriting; full drawing scans, photograph borders, source PDFs and video remain available.

## Finding

Point Reyes supplies a coherent architectural record for one real lighthouse: a central full-size Fresnel assembly in a glazed upper lantern, a clockwork room below, iron stair access and two exterior galleries. The strongest geometry owner is [LOC HABS CA-2250 / ca1357](https://www.loc.gov/item/ca1357/), sheet 1, read alongside the [official 1990 NPS architectural analysis](https://irma.nps.gov/DataStore/Reference/Profile/631767), printed pp. 95–101 (PDF pages 103–109). This is a specific building; no universal claim that every lighthouse is round is needed.

The packet is adequate architectural research, with real measured plans and distinct sides. It is **not yet a complete R271 scene-generation packet**: no textured roofless top/bird’s-eye of the lantern interior was found, and the available finished interior views do not give two wide opposing views of the upper lantern. No image was generated to hide either gap.

## What is physically supported

The optical visibility route is lamp → Fresnel optics → the glazed lantern perimeter → open sea. HABS-P3/P4 show the lens behind that glazing, and NPS-V93 shows the glass and sea directly beside the installed lens. Opaque walls around a tiny display lamp would not reproduce this source architecture.

The lower bridge enters the **clockwork level**. Its central mechanism supports the lens above; an interior stair reaches the upper lantern. Another stair connects ground level to the clockwork level. These connections are visible in sheet 1 and corroborated by the ground stair photograph, the 2020 entry sequence and the NPS report. The exterior upper gallery and its historic ladder route are distinct from these interior stairs. The 1990 report explicitly records removal of an exterior ladder: proposed/historic ladder details must not become an undocumented current feature.

The second-gallery plan locates the central lens within the glazed perimeter. The useful staging shape is the remaining circulation around that assembly, with access consuming part of the floor. Exact clearances have not been measured from these images. Do not assign invented metres or calibrated camera heights. Cabinets, service equipment and opaque paneling documented in the lower clockwork room must not be moved into the glazed lantern by combining references from different levels.

## Same place, different states

All ten HABS photos share survey CA-2250, and the original caption sheet dates them May 1982. The five measured sheets share the same survey. Several sheets explicitly label proposed 1982 restoration work; the exact drafting date is not established. The scans also carry an editorial caveat that insufficient information during editing may prevent HABS/HAER standards compliance. Retain the drawings as authoritative source evidence with that qualification, not certified current construction documents.

The 1990 NPS report describes the same building but predates the 2018–2019 restoration. [NPS documents](https://www.nps.gov/pore/planyourvisit/lighthouse.htm) temporary removal/refurbishment of the lens and clockwork, reconstruction of lantern/roof and replacement of aged Plexiglas with transparent glass. The 2020 virtual visit therefore supplies a separate restored appearance state. It is an edited visit with dissolves, not a continuous measured survey. Its brown plank-pattern clockwork floor differs from the resilient floor described in 1990; no material or chronology was invented to reconcile that appearance.

Four exact NPS album originals are retained: asset 3d0a37ab-9b32-4644-931c-7d145c90b995 is the restored exterior on 2019-10-21; assets 87b79674-d136-473d-bbf0-95c16870a6da and 0afbda2c-d3d9-49d3-9b1f-5211f6b4337f show the pedestal/window-frame work on 2019-06-15; asset 39fe4e0c-7dec-450b-8f89-1e54d430e034 shows a lens-less lantern on 2018-09-14. The latter three are construction references only. Their absent lens, scaffolding and protective covers must not be merged into a finished interior.

HABS-P9/P10 show the **separate fog-signal building**, not the lantern. The equipment building, visitor center and oil house are distinct structures. All ten archive photos are retained to preserve context; those two room interiors are explicitly excluded from lantern geometry.

Point Arena was not selected because its lens was moved to the museum during restoration, creating an unnecessary installation/state mismatch. No Point Arena, Split Rock or generic-lighthouse pixels enter the selected packet.
